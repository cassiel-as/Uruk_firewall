[ PHASE 03 — EIGHT-LAW INPUT MATRIX ]

  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  LAW 5 · SCIENCE · PRECISION
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  GDP is a precise measurement of a precisely
  defined thing: the monetary value of all
  final goods and services produced in an economy
  in a given period. Its precision is not in
  question. The precision failure is categorical:
  GDP is being used to measure something it was
  not designed to measure and cannot measure.
  Using GDP as a prosperity metric is equivalent
  to using a thermometer to measure blood pressure —
  the thermometer is precise, but it is measuring
  the wrong variable. The Easterlin Paradox
  (1974) demonstrated this categorically:
  above a threshold income level, GDP growth
  and reported wellbeing are uncorrelated.
  This finding is 50 years old and is taught
  in economics undergraduate courses. It has
  not changed the primary policy target of any
  major economy. The scientific precision failure
  is acknowledged inside the discipline and
  ignored outside it.

  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  LAW 4 · CHEMISTRY · TRANSFORMATION
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  GDP's structural indifference to the nature of
  transactions produces a systematic transformation
  of incentives. Because disasters, illness, crime,
  and pollution generate economic activity, GDP
  growth can be achieved by increasing the
  pathologies it purports to solve. This is not
  a theoretical problem. The fossil fuel industry
  contributes positively to GDP while its
  externalities (climate change, health impacts)
  generate further GDP through healthcare and
  disaster response. Natural capital depletion
  (deforestation, overfishing, aquifer drawdown)
  contributes positively to GDP while the future
  productive capacity it destroys is not counted
  as a cost. GDP is a metric that rewards the
  consumption of its own foundation. This
  transformation cost is invisible because
  natural capital and future productive capacity
  sit outside the measurement framework's boundary.

  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  LAW 6 · Philosophy:
  GDP embeds a philosophical claim: that value
  is equivalent to market price. Non-market
  activities (care, community, ecosystem services)
  are assigned zero value in the national accounts.
  This is a Benthamite utilitarian accounting
  decision made in 1944 that has been treated
  as a neutral technical standard ever since.

  LAW 1 · Art:
  "Economic growth" is the dominant metaphor of
  modernity — biological, directional, positive.
  Degrowth, steady-state, or alternative metrics
  are linguistically framed as subtraction.
  The metaphor forecloses non-growth pathways
  before policy analysis begins.

  LAW 2 · Psychology:
  GDP provides a single number that politicians
  can be held accountable to — simple, comparable,
  quarterly. Alternative wellbeing metrics are
  multi-dimensional and resist simple
  accountability framing. The cognitive demand
  of complexity sustains GDP's political
  dominance regardless of its measurement validity.

  LAW 3 · Physics:
  Infinite growth on a finite planet violates
  thermodynamic constraints. Material throughput
  required to sustain GDP growth on current
  trajectories will exceed planetary boundary
  limits within this century. GDP growth and
  planetary stability are physically incompatible
  at current material intensity — not politically
  incompatible, physically incompatible.

  LAW 7 · Geography:
  GDP growth in high-income countries often occurs
  through offshoring environmental and social
  costs to lower-income countries. The metric
  counts the value created in the transaction
  without counting the cost location.
  Global GDP can grow while aggregate global
  wellbeing and ecological stability decline —
  because the growth and the cost occur in
  different geographic jurisdictions and are
  measured separately.

  LAW 8 · Religion:
  GDP growth has become the secular religion
  of modern governance. Politicians of all
  ideological orientations pursue it.
  Questioning it is politically equivalent
  to questioning progress itself. The metric
  has acquired the properties of a sacred
  quantity — it organises collective behaviour,
  generates ritual reporting (quarterly GDP
  announcements), and its decline triggers
  collective anxiety responses that are
  disproportionate to actual wellbeing impact.

  national GDP accounting is production-based,
  not consumption-based. The geographic boundary
  of the metric systematically excludes the costs
  that occur outside it. This is a measurement
  design choice, not a data availability problem.
